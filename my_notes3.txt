Not exactly! 😊 You're close, but there is an important difference between browser autofill and cookies.

When you open a website and see a pop-up suggesting your username, that is usually the browser's autofill feature, not cookie-parser.

1. What happens when you log in?
html - How to change input text focus border - Stack Overflow

A. Browser autofill

Your browser remembers usernames and may offer to fill them in automatically. Passwords can be stored in its password manager.

User authentication for a Chrome Extension using a Django web app | by Matt Brown | Medium

B. Cookies

After login, a website may store a cookie in your browser so it can recognize your session on later requests.

Close-up of Express.js middleware setup in JavaScript backend, showing secure configuration using Helmet, CORS, Morgan, and body parsers for API development.

C. cookie-parser

This is backend middleware. It reads cookies sent by the browser and makes their values accessible to your Express application.

2. How they work together
Imagine you're building a login system using Express.js.

You enter your username and password.

Your browser may autofill these details if you've saved them.

Your server verifies your credentials and may create a session.

The server sends a session ID in a cookie.

On future requests, the browser sends that cookie.

cookie-parser can read the cookie on your backend.
